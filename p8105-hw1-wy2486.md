As shown above, calculating the mean of the character vector and factor
vector produces *NA* (does not work), while it works for the random
sample and logical vector. The random sample is numeric, so its mean can
be calculated directly. For the logical vector, R treats *TRUE as 1* and
*FALSE as 0*, so the mean can also be calculated. The character and
factor vectors are not numeric, so their means cannot be calculated
directly.

**Converting Variables From One Type To Another:**

``` r
as.numeric(pull(p2flippercat_df, logical_vector))

as.numeric(pull(p2flippercat_df, character_vector))

as.numeric(pull(p2flippercat_df, factor_vector))
```

When I ran the code chunk above, I found that:

- For logical_vector, R converts *TRUE* to 1 and *FALSE* to 0.
- For character_vector, R cannot convert values like *“Penguin_A”,
  “Penguin_B”, “Penguin_C”, etc* into numbers, so the results are NA.
- For factor_vector, R converts the categories into numbers, where
  *“long”* = 1, *“medium”* = 2, and *“short”* = 3. However, these
  numbers are just codes for the different categories and do not have
  actual numerical meaning.

This helps explain the results when calculating the mean. The mean works
for the logical vector because R can treat *TRUE* and *FALSE* as 1 and
0. However, a mean does not make sense for the character or factor
vectors because they do not contain actual numerical measurements.
